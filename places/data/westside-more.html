The Westside refers to areas west of Central LA, which include over a dozen inland and coastal LA city neighborhoods and three smaller cities – Santa Monica, Culver City, and Beverly Hills – sitting fully within LA city borders. Compared to the eclectic urban neighborhoods of Central LA with recognizable landmarks, much of inland Westside feels closer to LA's upscale suburbia, yet coexisting with the towering corporate office buildings of Century City and Westwood. It's as you head further west, however, that you'll begin to tangibly feel what really draws crowds from all over the world to this particular half of the city. Temperatures become milder – cooler in the summer and warmer in the winter – and you'll sense the ocean breeze in the air as you approach the coastline of Santa Monica and LA city – Playa del Rey, Venice, Marina del Rey and the Pacific Palisades – each with a unique interpretation of what LA coastal life looks like.

Head north from the Westside on the Pacific Coast Highway for refreshing coastal hikes in the Santa Monica Mountains from Topanga or Malibu, which take up LA County's final coastline until reaching the coast of Ventura County – home to Ojai Valley and some of the most scenic hidden gem coastal towns in Greater LA with striking views of the Channel Islands National Park . Finally, north of Ventura lies Santa Barbara, an Angeleno-favorite getaway known for its Spanish Revival architecture.
