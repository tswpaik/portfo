Tokyo (東京) is the heart of Japan, anchoring the country as its central hub for politics, finance, media, pop culture, and global influence. With its heavily developed urban facade, it's easy to forget that Tokyo dates back to the 15th century as a city called Edo developed by the Tokugawa shogunate during the Edo Period (1603-1868) . Renamed Tokyo – "the Eastern Capital" – in 1868, the city has been modern Japan's capital for over 150 years, taking the baton from its ancient capital Kyoto in the west and shifting the center of the country from the historic Kansai region to its younger counterpart Kanto, now Japan's most densely populated region by far, with over 40 million residents.

What makes Tokyo so compelling to many across the world is how seamlessly it blends the best of traditional and modern Japan: centuries-old temples, like Senso-ji founded in 628, stand just a train ride from the neon-lit scramble of Shibuya Crossing , while serene gardens and historic neighborhoods coexist with cutting-edge architecture, a complex network of trains, and a diverse and ever-evolving culinary and arts scene. No matter how many times you visit, Tokyo is just one of those world-class cities that has something new to offer each time.

Vibes at a Glance

Favorite Stuff to Do

  • Take in Tokyo from high up: Tokyo has many iconic skyscrapers with observation decks, like Tokyo Tower and Skytree, but I think a truly "great" view of Tokyo should have these iconic buildings in the frame along with Tokyo's many other landmarks. In that sense, Shibuya Sky takes the cake with its outdoor rooftop looking onto every single Tokyo landmark – from Shibuya Crossing, Tokyo Tower, the Skytree, Tokyo Bay and even the top of Mount Fuji on a clear day. The views here are nothing short of surreal.
  • Walk through Meiji Jingu's forest: Escape the chaos of Shinjuku or Harajuku and step into 170 acres of tranquil forest with ancient trees tall enough to basically cover the sky with a green canopy, culminating to one of Japan's most serene shrines. Don't miss the museum.
  • Watch Kabuki at Ginza: The centuries-old art of Kabuki, even today, is truly one of the most unique experiences you can have in the world. Reserve a single act ticket for a show at Ginza's Kabukiza built in 1889, which even has captioning devices available for non-Japanese speakers.
  • Take in Tokyo Bay from Odaiba: While most know Odaiba for its shopping malls, the name originates from its Daiba, or battery islands, built in Tokyo Bay in the 1850s to defend the city against Commodore Perry. You can easily walk over to one of these remote islands from Odaiba Seaside Park for an escape from the city and take in the breeze and views of Rainbow Bridge and Tokyo Tower across the bay.
  • Stroll around Ueno Park: Ueno Park is one of the oldest parks in the city and feels like the Central Park of Tokyo, with its many museums, street performers, outdoor festivals, and locals enjoying its wide green spaces.
  • Go listen to jazz: Tokyo is one of the jazz capitals of the world, both in terms of fans of the music and professional musicians. For fellow jazz fans, go to Jazz Spot Intro in Takadanobaba or Ems in Ginza for live sessions, or drop by Shinjuku's legendary Dug to listen to their record collection.
  • Enjoy a rooftop footbath with panoramic bay views: Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai is a dining/shopping complex by the new fish market, but its best-kept secret is the rooftop footbath with panoramic views of Tokyo Bay. Nothing like relaxing your tired traveler feet while watching a Tokyo Bay sunset.
  • Decompress at an open-air Onsen: Walking distance from Sugamo Station off the Yamanote, Tokyo Somei Onsen Sakura offers peaceful outdoor baths where you can enjoy the breeze while relaxing in hot spring water from Toshima. Then, close out with a cold beer or shaved ice at their cafe.
  • Enjoy the seasons: If you're lucky enough to be in Tokyo during spring or fall, my favorite places to be are Meguro River and Mount Takao, respectively. Meguro River is lined with Sakura trees that get lit up at night, drawing crowds from the whole city, while autumn simply begs for a hike to Mount Takao for its fall folliage and views.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • Marunouchi & Yaesu: Take in Tokyo Station from outside, with the Marunouchi side boasting its classic facade from 1914 and Yaesu with its futuristic vibes. Both sides are lined with some of Tokyo's best restaurants and bars, with night time views everywhere. For me, chilly Tokyo evenings call for Suage soup curry at Marunouchi Brick Square.
  • Yurakucho: Go to Yurakucho for atmospheric bars and eateries nestled under Tokyo's raised train tracks, where you'll be greeted with Yakitori smoke and the sound of beer glasses clinking. If you've had too many beers or Chuhai drinks in Japan, the Stand in Yurakucho is one of my favorite bars in Tokyo for whiskey-based cocktails.
  • Ryogoku Station: Eat (healthy) like a sumo at Ryogoku, home to Japan's largest and most important sumo arena, and more importantly, eateries that serve Chanko Nabe, a hearty hot pot dish that every sumo eats – supposedly the secret to their strength, but just plain good.
  • Lunch at Tsukiji Market & dessert in an Edo era garden: While the main fish market has moved to Toyosu, the Tsukiji Outer Market still remains a great place for fresh Sushi, Chirashi, and Kaisen-don. Walk over to nearby Hamarikyu, an impressive garden that was home to an Edo Period feudal lord, for Matcha with Wagashi.
  • Yakatabune dinner cruise: Reserve a traditional Japanese dinner on a Yakatabune for a dining experience that dates back to the Heian Period. Boats depart from Sumida River or Shinagawa and travel through Tokyo's many waterways out to the Tokyo Bay with Rainbow Bridge views. It includes Nomi-hodai, all-you-can-drink, so do pace yourself.
  • Tachi-zushi & Tachi-nomi: Enjoy great deals on good Sushi or drinks at one of Tokyo's many standing spots. While not unique to Tokyo, you're likely to find these spots in or around stations in big cities, frequented by locals in transit. For Sushi, look for a Nihon-ichi Uogashi.
  • Tokyo Aburagumi Souhonten: A newer local specialty, Tokyo Abura Soba is essentially a soupless ramen, where the broth is replaced by a soy-based sauce with sesame and chili oils, vinegar, and a variety of toppings. You can add the various condiments available and customize until you arrive at the exact flavor you want.
  • Tsukishima Monja Street & Asakusa Hoppy Street: These two streets are known to be lined with no-frills eateries serving Tokyo's local soul foods and affordable drinks. Monja-yaki is Tokyo's specialty that tastes better than it looks, and Hoppy Street is known for Gyusuji or Motsu Nikomi, stewed beef tendon or pork offal.
