Just across the LA River from Central LA neighborhoods like Los Feliz, Silver Lake and Elysian Park, Northeast LA is a hidden gem of the city that blends nature with a vibrant arts and food scene. Areas nestled in lush hills like Montecito Heights, Monterey Hills, and Mount Washington offer sweeping views of the Downtown LA skyline from unique angles unfamiliar to many, while the revitalized LA River with relaxed riverside trails and green spaces is a key feature of Atwater Village and Cypress Park, alongside Central LA's Frogtown. In Highland Park, Eagle Rock, and Glassell Park, you'll find one-of-a-kind restaurants, bars, and arts venues that reflect the city's creative, ever-evolving culture.

Neighbors to the south, the Eastside refers to some of LA's most historic neighborhoods directly east of Downtown LA: Lincoln Heights, Boyle Heights, El Sereno, and the unincorporated area of East Los Angeles. The Eastside is proudly shaped by a long history of diverse immigrants and today the heart of LA's Chicano culture, community, and activism. Known for vibrant murals, authentic Mexican food, historic architecture, and a strong sense of community, the Eastside is anchored by Whittier Boulevard, which transitions into the new 3,500-feet-long Sixth Street Bridge – a modern civil landmark opened in 2022 that connects the Eastside to the Arts District with panoramic views of LA's skyline.

Another standout feature of Northeast LA and the Eastside is the region's direct access to Metro's best light rails to Downtown LA, revamped by the Regional Connector Project completed in 2023. The A and E lines, serving Northeast LA and the Eastside respectively, puts the region within a quick train ride to all the attractions located along Downtown's Metro stops: Chinatown Station for iconic LA eateries and the city's sprawling LA State Historic Park; Union Station for a stroll to Olvera Street or transfers to the B and D lines (serving Koreatown, Hollywood, Mid-Wilshire and Fairfax), direct buses to Dodger Stadium and LAX, as well as the scenic Pacific Surfliner trains that run along the Southern California coast to Ventura, Santa Barbara, or San Diego; Little Tokyo / Arts District Station for traditional and contemporary dining options that exist side-by-side; Historic Broadway Station for Grand Central Market and LA's Historic Core; Grand Ave Arts / Bunker Hill Station for the Broad Museum, MOCA, Walt Disney Concert Hall, Music Center, Grand Park and City Hall; 7th / Metro Center Station for the modern urban amenities of the Financial District; and finally, Pico Station for entertainment at South Park's LA Live, Staples Center and the Grammy Museum.

With soaring skyline views to admire along the way, a ride downtown only takes 15 minutes from Northeast LA's Highland Park Station or Atlantic Station between East LA and Monterey Park. And to further sweeten the deal, Angelenos can now set out from Northeast LA or the Eastside on these two light rails, continue past Pico Station through Downtown, all the way to Santa Monica or Long Beach for the flat rate of $1.75 – completely busting the outdated myth that you can't do LA without a car, freeway traffic, or parking hassles.
