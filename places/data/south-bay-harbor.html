The South Bay and Harbor Region include communities along the lower half of the Santa Monica Bay, typically thought of as south of LAX, all the way down to the Palos Verdes Peninsula and the county's southern coast where the LA Harbor sits.

The look-and-feel of this region feels exceptionally diverse. Along the top half of the South Bay, you'll find laid-back beaches similar to the coastline of LA's Westside, with a long uninterrupted "strand" of beachfront and bike paths shared by Manhattan, Hermosa and Redondo Beach, home to LA County's largest pier with a unique horseshoe shape. My personal favorite stretch of coastline in all of LA, however, is further south, where the scenery shifts to striking cliffs and rugged shorelines with elevated coastal trails that have sweeping views directly facing Catalina Island, a defining feature of the Palos Verdes Peninsula and Coastal San Pedro area. At the end of LA's coast is the Port of LA and Long Beach, together called the LA Harbor – the largest and busiest harbor in the US, nicknamed "America's Port."

The region has more to offer beyond its beautiful coast: authentic Asian restaurants and markets to explore in and around Gardena and Torrance, home to the largest Japanese community in the Mainland US, and lively restaurants and world-class music/sports venues in the Inglewood area by LAX.
