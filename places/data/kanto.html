With a population of over 42 million, Kanto (関東) is Japan's most densely populated region with seven prefectures: Tokyo-to, Kanagawa, Chiba, Saitama, Ibaraki, Tochigi, and Gunma. Beyond Japan, the region is consistently among the top three most populated metro areas in the world. At its core is Tokyo, Japan's capital since 1868 – originally Edo – and the center of Japanese politics, finance, and pop culture.

While Tokyo is often seen as a single city, it is actually a prefecture-like area called a "to" (都), starting with the 23 special wards near the Tokyo Bay – the part often regarded as "the city" with 10 million residents there alone. Tokyo-to, however, reaches far inland including over 30 more cities, towns, and even mountain villages.

Despite Tokyo-to's enormity on its own, it is actually the entirety of Kanto that is considered Shutoken – the Capital Region or Greater Tokyo – since the other six prefectures are in reach of Tokyo by public transit. One such gem of Kanto – with a mix of urban, traditional, and natural sights to explore – is Kanagawa .

Kanagawa prefecture is home to Yokohama – one of Japan's most important ports that has developed into the country's second largest city, surpassing the population of Japan's age-old historic second city Osaka in 1980. Further south, you'll find Kamakura, a former Shogunate capital famous for its well-preserved temples, shrines, and trails, along with the scenery of Sagami Bay and Hakone, which offer views of Mount Fuji on clear days.
