Historic LA at a Glance

There's often a misconception – admittedly held by locals and non-locals alike – that LA is too young, spread out, or quick to change to have well-preserved historic architecture that rivals other major American cities. While this might ring true for much of LA's younger neighborhoods or suburban sprawl, Downtown LA – the original core of the city and region – has all the proof to debunk that myth.

Sitting north of City Hall, El Pueblo de Los Angeles is the birthplace of LA founded in 1781. ${new Date().getFullYear() - 1781} years later, it still stands in its original spot, honoring the city's history as a monument with 27 well-preserved buildings, including the ${new Date().getFullYear() - 1822}-year-old Church of Our Lady Queen of the Angels . The Historic Core , in particular, is also a treasure trove of architecturally elaborate buildings and pre-Hollywood era movie palaces in the Broadway Theater District , lined with buildings primarily built between the late 1800s and mid 1900s, preserved with local and national landmark status designations. Boasting a range of classic architectural styles like Spanish Baroque, Italian Renaissance Revival, Beaux-Arts, Art Deco, and more, Downtown LA is one of the few places in the expansive Greater LA region that can give you a glimpse of historic Angeleno craftsmanship and architectural excellence from LA's classic film-like past.

That said, Downtown LA's urban architecture is not only historic but also the most modern, innovative, and iconic in the city of LA. The city's skyline is virtually defined by two of the tallest skyscrapers not only in LA or California but the western half of the US: specifically, the US Bank Tower (1989) and its newer rival, the Wilshire Grand Center (2017) . Nestled among the many other skyscrapers of Bunker Hill , you'll also find some of the most experimental buildings in LA along Grand Avenue, housing LA's world-class music and arts institutions like the LA Music Center , Walt Disney Concert Hall and the Grand LA complex by Frank Gehry, the Broad Museum , Museum of Contemporary Art (MOCA) , and the Colburn School of Music , all offering a wide range of free exhibitions, concerts and community events, and together host the annual Grand Ave Arts All Access event.

Of course, Downtown LA is much more than its impressive buildings. It is home to a diverse community of Angelenos and particularly two of LA's earliest Asian immigrant communities that date back to the 1870s and 1880s respectively: historic Chinatown , lined with some of the city's oldest and most celebrated classic Chinese restaurants like Yang Chow and the Golden Dragon, and perhaps Downtown LA's most lively, bustling district, Little Tokyo – one of the country's largest Japanese-American neighborhoods steeped in history and tradition that continue to this day, with annual Obon festivals held across its many Buddhist temples during mid-to-late July and the grand Nisei Week festivities in August. Just east of Little Tokyo is also Downtown LA's Arts District , a vibrant artist neighborhood that has become a hub for galleries, murals, and most notably, trendy restaurants and bars.

Perhaps the most unexpected and overlooked feature of Downtown LA – again, for locals and non-locals alike – is its new green spaces. Over the years, the city and state have delivered on several beautiful and well-maintained green spaces for the community in and around downtown, where greenery has historically been lacking. Two of the most notable are Grand Park (2012) , which connects City Hall to the LA Music Center with a modern park space that matches its urban surroundings, and LA State Historic Park (2017) just outside of Chinatown, 30+ acres of grassy fields and tree-lined paths for walking and biking, with striking views of downtown's skyline. As a regular, I can say LA State Historic Park is definitely one of the most widely used parks in the city and feels closest to New York's Central Park, a much needed breath of fresh air and nature in a bustling urban jungle.

Favorite Stuff to Do

  • LA State Historic Park: Walk your dog, check out the farmers market on Thursdays, or just go for the views.
  • City Hall Observation Deck: One of the best free views of LA from the 27th floor. It'll feel like a best-kept secret.
  • Union Station: Take a train to Santa Barbara, pre-game at Traxx before a Dodgers game, or just visit for the many exhibitions and events they hold here. Either way, just being inside the station is a treat in and of itself.
  • Grand Performances Summer Concerts at Cal Plaza: Enjoy LA summer nights with free concerts by world-class musicians, surrounded by Bunker Hill skyscrapers.
  • Live Jazz at the Biltmore Hotel: Every Thursday 6-8 pm at the Gallery Bar. Jazz in a venue with historic detail in every corner and great cocktails – hard one to beat.
  • Live Jazz at Garçons de Café: Food and drinks are just icing on the cake for live jazz in the Arcade Building.
  • Movie with drinks at Alamo Drafthouse at the Bloc: Yep, here you can have the drinks while you watch the movie.
  • Rooftop Cinema Club DTLA: Watch a movie on the rooftop of an LA high-rise surrounded by city lights.
  • Walt Disney Concert Hall: Any concert here is great, but the Halloween silent films accompanied by an organist are just fun. Don't miss the Blue Ribbon Garden.
  • The Broad Museum: World-class art for free. Reserve tickets to Yayoi Kusuma's Infinity Room beforehand.
  • Last Bookstore: It's a bookstore, a record store, but also a labyrinth with secret rooms, in an old bank building.
  • Coffee at the Bradbury & Eastern Columbia: Get a coffee at the Bradbury's Blue Bottle or Eastern Columbia's ilcaffè for an excuse to marvel at LA's historic buildings.
  • Apple Tower Theatre: Not your typical Apple Store. Step into a restored original LA movie palace built in 1927.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • Philippe & Cole's: French Dip = LA = French Dip. My take is Philppe for lunch, Cole's for dinner and cocktails.
  • Highland Park Brewery: Across the street from LA State Historic Park, this is hands-down the most relaxed place to have a beer outdoors with a view of LA's skyline.
  • Golden Dragon Restuarant: Just across from Central Plaza, classic Dimsum hall with old-school vibes.
  • El Paseo Inn: Sit outside with a margarita and table-side guacamole and soak in the atmosphere of Olvera Street.
  • Grand Central Market: Go on a weekday, and you can enjoy it in peace with the locals. Tons of great options, but Tacos Tumbras a Tomas is one of LA's best deals.
  • ,Perch: The OG rooftop bar that basically started the scene, yet still the best view and best happy hour deal.
  • The Wolves: Surreal bar in the Historic Core built with authentic parts from an early 1900s French train station.
  • Bottega Louie: On busy nights, you'll need to scream across the table just to be heard, but still a DTLA classic.
  • Izakaya Bizan: Warm chochin lights, Yakitori grill smoke, 2000s J-pop – no frills, just like the Izakayas in Japan.
  • ,Kushi Shabu: Sit at the counter and get the Kushikatsu over Shabu. Their skewers are deep-fried to perfection.
  • Torigoya: If you're serious about Yakitori, this is your spot. Don't miss their special homemade ginger sauce.
  • Far Bar: Enjoy drinks outdoors in a narrow atmospheric brick wall alley next to Little Tokyo's Far East building.
  • Pine & Crane DTLA: South Park location for Silver Lake's famed Taiwanese spot but with a more urban, sleek vibe.
  • Everson Royce: Cocktails and small plates in a hidden courtyard outdoors, tucked away in the Arts District.
  • Smorgasburg LA: Avoid hot days because you could grill on egg on the venue's asphalt lot. Still, the birria tacos from Goat Mafia and kava drinks make it worth the trip.
  • Langer's Deli, Westlake: For true foodies, #19 is a legend you'll need to try. It's not in DTLA, but it is just a 5-minute drive out of DTLA's Financial District on 7th.
