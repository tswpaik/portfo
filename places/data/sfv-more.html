Called exclusively "the Valley" despite LA's many other valleys, the San Fernando Valley is perhaps the most iconic LA suburbia. Its image as LA's quintessential suburbs has been shaped by decades of TV and film, often focusing on its mall culture, car-centric life, and the Valley Girl persona. While other LA suburban areas consist of independent cities or unincorporated areas, most of the Valley is officially part of the city of LA , creating a closer connection with the city's culture and identity. Also unlike many LA suburbs, the Valley sits closer to Hollywood rather than Downtown LA, making it a natural home base for studios and those in Hollywood's entertainment industries. Even local performing arts venues here, like CSUN's Soraya , are often a popular stop for world-famous artists touring in LA.

The region, however, has much more to offer beyond its suburban connection to the film and entertainment industries of Hollywood. Reflecting LA and its cosmopolitan culture, the Valley is also home to a multicultural and open-minded population, most notably with a thriving food scene that celebrates diversity.

Offering one of the few permanent open-air food markets in LA besides Farmers Market on 3rd and Smorgasburg, the Wat Thai Temple located in North Hollywood is a prime example. As LA's largest Thai Buddhist temple, Wat Thai serves as a gathering place every weekend for LA's Thai community, with food vendors that also welcome Angelenos of all backgrounds from across the city with some of the most authentic and affordable Thai food that rivals East Hollywood's Thai Town in Central LA.

Lesser-known fact, the hillside of Sherman Oaks, Encino, and Tarzana is actually part of the Santa Monica Mountains , just across the mountain range from their LA Basin-side counterparts like Beverly Crest, Bel-Air, and Brentwood. That being the case, these areas also have some great hiking trails owned by the Santa Monica Mountains Conservancy, offering sweeping views of the entire Valley and the LA Basin. Around its western edges, the Valley also boasts direct access to some of LA's most unique natural scenery, like the rolling hills of the Upper Las Virgenes Canyon in Woodland Hills and Cretaceous Period sandstone formations in Chatsworth. Finally, bordering the Valley to the north is yet another pair of valley communities of LA County: Santa Clarita Valley – home to one of LA's newer suburban developments and Castaic Lake – and the Antelope Valley, known for its Mojave desert landscapes and California Poppy Reserve.
