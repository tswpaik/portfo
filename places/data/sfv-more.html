Claiming "the Valley" as its title despite LA's many valleys, the San Fernando Valley is probably what most picture as classic LA suburbia. Its strong image as "the" suburbs of LA has been cemented by decades of TV and film, portraying its car-centric mall culture, tract housing, and the Valley Girl persona. In contrast to South Bay and SGV suburbs, primarily consisting of older independent cities, the Valley was largely developed post-World War II and is mostly under LA city jurisdiction , while still perceived as separate from the city. Also unlike most LA suburbs, the Valley sits closer to Hollywood rather than Downtown LA, making it a natural overflow for Hollywood's studios and industries. Even local concert venues here, like CSUN's Soraya , often host famous artists on tour.

The region, however, has more to offer beyond its connection to the film and entertainment industries of Hollywood. Reflecting LA and its cosmopolitan culture, the Valley is also home to a multicultural population, most notably with a thriving food scene that celebrates diversity.

Offering one of the few permanent open-air food markets in LA besides Farmers Market on 3rd and Smorgasburg, the Wat Thai Temple located in North Hollywood is a prime example. As LA's largest Thai Buddhist temple, Wat Thai serves as a gathering place every weekend for LA's Thai community, with food vendors that also welcome Angelenos of all backgrounds from across the city with some of the most authentic and affordable Thai food that rivals Central LA's Thai Town.

What perhaps should be a more obvious fact that often comes as a surprise to Angelenos in the basin is that the southern hillside of the Valley in Sherman Oaks, Encino, and Tarzana are actually on the Santa Monica Mountains , just across the mountain range from their widely recognized basin-side counterparts like Beverly Crest, Bel-Air, and Brentwood. That being the case, these areas also have several great trails owned by the Santa Monica Mountains Conservancy, offering sweeping views of the Valley but also the entire basin and coast. Around its western edges, the Valley also boasts direct access to some of LA's most untouched nature, like the rolling hills of the Upper Las Virgenes Canyon in Woodland Hills and Cretaceous Period sandstone formations in Chatsworth. Finally, bordering the Valley to the north is yet another pair of valley communities of LA County: Santa Clarita Valley – home to one of LA's newer suburban developments and Castaic Lake – and Antelope Valley, known for Mojave desert landscapes and California Poppy reserves.
