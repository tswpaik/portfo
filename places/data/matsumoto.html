Traveling across Japan, it's not uncommon, even today, to spot attention-grabbing castles perched on a hill or nestled in the heart of a city, but the vast majority are actually modern reconstructions built in the 20th century to represent local pride and the city or region's prosperous feudal past – at times, domestically criticized for their varying levels of authenticity, lack of attention to detail, or excessively tourist-geared intentions. By contrast, Matsumoto Castle (松本城) in Nagano prefecture – a National Treasure of Japan – is one of 12 Genson Tenshu (現存天守) , certified authentic castles with their original keep intact since their construction in the feudal period. Built in 1594, this five-tier six-story castle, reaching almost 100 feet in height, stands as proof to Japan's feudal history and the period's architectural prowess.

Set against the dramatic backdrop of the Japanese Alps of Chubu, Matsumoto Castle is often regarded among the most impressive even of the exclusive 12 originals, often mentioned as one of Japan's top two castles alongside Himeji Castle in Hyogo prefecture, which is almost a complete visual opposite of Matsumoto Castle. Nicknamed Karasu-jo, or the "Crow Castle," for its striking lacquered black exterior, Matsumoto Castle juxtaposes dark colors with red accents, such as its Uzumibashi Bridge and the railing of the Tsukimi Yagura, a small tower added to the castle with a deck specifically for viewing the moon – a rare, almost poetic feature found only in a few Japanese castles primarily built for warring Samurai lords and with defensive purposes in mind.
