Chubu (中部) translates to "the middle region" in Japanese, referring to the nine prefectures that sit between the Kanto and Kansai regions – put another way, Eastern and Western Japan. Chubu itself is divided into three sub-regions:

  • Koshin-etsu (Yamanashi, Nagano, and Niigata): mountainous prefectures housing the Japanese Alps and Mount Fuji, shared by Yamanashi and Shizuoka
  • Hokuriku (Ishikawa, Toyama, and Fukui): a central hub for traditional crafts and cultural heritage – Kanazawa being one of the best known cities for these elements
  • Tokai (Aichi, Gifu, and Shizuoka): an industrially developed major population corridor along the Pacific

As Japan's fourth largest city by population and one of the largest industrial hubs in the country, Nagoya of Aichi Prefecture is the undisputed urban and economic powerhouse that anchors the region. Historically, the city was also the seat of the famed Owari Tokugawa Family , with Nagoya Castle symbolizing its role in shaping early modern Japan, and that legacy of power and production continues today through its position at the heart of Japan's manufacturing economy, housing Toyota headquarters and much of the broader automotive industry. Culturally, the city is also known for its distinct food scene, often called Nagoya Meshi , featuring rich, bold flavors such as Hitsumabushi (grilled eel over rice), Tebasaki chicken wings, Miso Katsu, Kishimen (flat noodles), and other Miso-based dishes that set the city's local flavors noticeably apart from the rest of the country.
