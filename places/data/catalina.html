A classic LA County gem, Santa Catalina Island – or simply Catalina – sits just 20 miles off of LA's southern coast and is an easy hour-long ferry ride from the LA Harbor Region. It is one of the eight Channel Islands of California but by far the most well-known with a long history as LA's vacation destination since the 1880s. While the other islands are remote and uninhabited, five forming the Channel Islands National Park accessible from Ventura, Catalina blends its nature and landscapes with the amenities of a small relaxed island town, making it a popular getaway for those looking to escape the city's hustle and bustle.

The historic town of Avalon – sitting in a small, picturesque bay along the southeast coast of the island – lines its clear beaches with colorful buildings that dot the steep slopes of the nearby hills. Its compact downtown is easy to walk around and mixes 1920s Art Deco and Mediterranean Revival architecture like the Catalina Casino with coastal bungalows and a relaxed waterfront.

Catalina is also a destination that calls out to hikers with its scenic hiking trails. The famous Trans-Catalina Trail begins in Avalon Canyon and – over several days – leads to Lands End , the island's remote West End, made famous as 2019's macOS wallpaper. Luckily, the trail also branches off to stunning shorter side trails, quite a few of them on the East End by Avalon, offering views of the San Pedro Channel with LA's mainland coast on one side and the open Pacific Ocean with San Clemente Island on the other – views you can't see anywhere on LA's mainland.
