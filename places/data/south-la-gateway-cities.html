South LA is home to many historic and culturally significant institutions in the city of LA. Developed in the 1870s to host some of the largest fairs in the state and country, Exposition Park served as the primary stage for the 1932 and 1984 LA Summer Olympics centered around the LA Memorial Coliseum, where the 2028 LA Summer Olympics will kick off, making it the first stadium in the world to host the Olympics for a third time. The park is also a cultural anchor with world-class museums and institutions, like the Natural History Museum of LA County, California Science Center and the Lucas Museum of Narrative Art – the buzziest addition in 2026, long awaited by Star Wars and cinema buffs.

South LA has also been the long-standing historic and cultural home for LA's Black community and a vibrant generational hub for Black American arts, music, and activism. Exposition Park's California African American Museum (CAAM) preserves and elevates the stories of Black Angelenos, while adjacent neighborhoods like Leimert Park and Historic South Central serve as vital creative centers for its diverse community, housing LA's most prominent jazz festivals and community-driven food scenes like Mercado La Paloma.

Just north of Exposition Park across Exposition Boulevard is the University of Southern California founded in 1880, the oldest university in LA and all of Southern California . USC plays a central role in connecting the city of LA to the world through education and research, attracting students and scholars from across the country and around the world – many of whom, including myself, choose to put down roots in LA after their time at SC. The surrounding neighborhood of University Park, much of which is designated a Historic Preservation Overlay Zone , is home to some of LA's best preserved Victorian, Craftsman, and Italian Renaissance Revival architecture, with historic mansions reflecting late 19th and early 20th century styles.

East of South LA, Southeast LA County is a vast region of 26 independent cities with strong industrial roots. The region often refers to itself as the Gateway Cities given its location along the 5 Santa Ana Freeway between LA and Orange County , acting as a transit / trade gateway between the two.

Cities like Montebello and Pico Rivera are known for their good food and old-school restaurants, and the Citadel Outlets of Commerce located right on the 5 freeway, catches the eyes of drivers on the 5 with its kitschy Assyrian Revival architecture, a well-preserved 1930s building that captures Hollywood's fascination with exotic, ancient motifs during the 1920s to 1930s, much like Grauman's Egyptian.
