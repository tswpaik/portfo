The Kansai (関西) region sits west of the Chubu and Kanto regions, consisting of seven prefectures: Kyoto, Shiga, Mie, Nara, Wakayama, Osaka, and Hyogo. Still commonly referenced by its old name Kinki (近畿), meaning "region near the capital," Kansai is the original capital region of ancient Japan, housing Kyoto, Japan's most recognized historic capital from 794 to 1868, along with its predecessor Nara, which served as the nation's first permanent capital from 710 to 784, making the region a focal point for Japanese history, culture, tradition, and proudly preserved ancient architecture such as temples, shrines, original castles like Himeji Castle, as well as Edo Period storefronts and residences still in use.

15 minutes east of Kyoto lies the crown jewel of Shiga and Kansai's nature: Lake Biwa, one of the world's most ancient lakes, estimated to be over 4 million years old, and the country's largest lake by far. Spanning 260 square miles, the lake is larger than all 23 special wards of Tokyo combined and three times the size of the country's historic second city, Osaka. In addition to its natural beauty, the lake and its surrounding region holds great historic significance and cultural heritage, both releated to and independent of its neighbor Kyoto. Sitting on the lake's southern shores, Shiga's capital Otsu also served as the interim imperial capital prior to Nara and was the east end of the Tokaido , Japan's historic route between Kyoto and Edo/Tokyo.

The significance of the Kansai region, however, doesn't stop at its ancient history. Today, Kansai is a major population and urban center in Japan, with a combined regional population of around 21 million people, forming the country's second-largest economic and metropolitan area after Greater Tokyo .

Kansai's core metropolitan area is called Keihanshin (京阪神) – each Kanji (Chinese character) signifying Kyoto, Osaka, and Kobe respectively — and serves as the region's hub for commerce, culture, and transportation. Osaka, the region's most populated urban area, is not only a commercial and industrial powerhouse but also one of the country's culture centers rivaling Tokyo. Known as the birthplace of Manzai , traditional Japanese comedy, Osaka and its community's fast-talking humor and down-to-earth culture is formative to Japan's entertainment industry.
