LA County's suburban sprawl is nothing short of ginormous, consisting of 87 cities beyond the city of LA and 101 unincorporated areas . Two of the largest suburban areas sit just beyond Downtown LA, bordering Northeast LA and the Eastside: the city of Glendale and the San Gabriel Valley (SGV) – the latter made of 36 cities and unincorporated areas , taking up most of eastern LA County.

Among the largest cities in the county, Glendale has a downtown that is considerably larger than the typical LA suburb, offering upscale shopping and dining options that draw crowds from LA, SGV and nearby communities in the Crescenta Valley. Located by the Verdugo Mountains which sit right between SGV and the San Fernando Valley, the city also has some great hiking trails with sweeping views of the LA Basin and is famous for its Mediterranean / Middle Eastern cuisine, being home to the largest Armenian community in the US.

Surrounded by the towering San Gabriel Mountains , the SGV region, on the other hand, is among the most historic places in the LA area. Founded in 1886 and home to the Rose Bowl , SGV's largest city Pasadena ties with Santa Monica as the second oldest city in LA County after the city of LA. More importantly, however, the region originates from Mission San Gabriel Arcángel, built in 1771 as the first mission in Greater LA and precursor to the founding of LA itself as a Pueblo in 1781, making it one of the most important sites in California history that you can easily see while in the area.

Today, much of SGV is most notably home to the largest Asian community in the US, resulting in one of the most celebrated Asian food scenes in the country – with everything from regional Chinese cuisines to a wide range of East and Southeast Asian food, drinks, and desserts readily available throughout the region.

SGV spans wide across eastern LA County, with cities like Monterey Park, Alhambra, and South Pasadena sitting right up against LA city's eastern borders blending the boundaries, while communities around the Puente Hills, East SGV, and cities further east in the Pomona Valley are often closer to LA's neighboring counties than they are to the city of LA, specifically west Inland Empire and/or north Orange County , both of which have their own culture and political leanings that differ from LA's.
