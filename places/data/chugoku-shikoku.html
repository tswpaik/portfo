Chugoku region (中国地方) and Shikoku (四国) surround the Seto Inland Sea – one of Japan's most scenic bodies of water. Chugoku lies on the main island Honshu north of the strait, while Shikoku, another of Japan's four main islands, sits south forming the strait itself.

Chugoku is also commonly subdivided into the regions of San-yo (Yamaguchi, Hiroshima, and Okayama prefectures) to the south along the Seto Inland Sea, characterized by milder weather, industry, and larger cities, versus San-in (Shimane and Tottori prefectures) to the north, known for the unique natural beauty of its rural landscapes and rugged coastline. Across the Seto Inland Sea, Shikoku houses four prefectures, Ehime, Kochi, Tokushima, and Kagawa – home to the Sanuki Udon , a Japanese staple found throughout the country.

The major cities anchoring this area are Hiroshima and Okayama. Known internationally for its history of having been devastated by an atomic bomb towards the end of World War II, the city of Hiroshima today serves as a center for remembrance and restoration of peace and hope, as the thriving, rebuilt urban center it is today. Off the city's shore along the Seto Inland Sea lies Miyajima and the floating Torii gate of Itsukushima Shrine, one of Nihon Sankei – Japan's three most scenic views. To the east, Okayama is known for its striking castle, often called Ujo or the "Black Bird Castle" – and Kouraku-en Garden, one of the famed Three Great Gardens of Japan , boasting a refined contrast of landscape design and traditional architecture.
