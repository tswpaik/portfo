Los Angeles – the largest city on the West Coast and second largest in the country – is one of only two megacities in the US. Unlike its counterpart New York City, known for its dense vertical development concentrated in the 25 square miles of Manhattan, LA is famous for its horizontal growth, filling close to 1,500 square miles of the LA Basin with urban sprawl right up to its vast coastline and spilling into numerous valleys further inland formed by LA's rugged mountains. Despite its unparalleled sprawl, LA does have an original urban core with rich historic and cultural significance.

Just under 60 square miles, Central LA sits near the geographic center of the city and county. In its southeast corner is Downtown LA, the birthplace of LA as a Pueblo in 1781 and the region's political heart, housing City Hall, which represents a city of 4 million Angelenos, and the main government buildings for LA County – the most populous, diverse county in the US with almost 10 million residents, surpassing 40 states in population as a single county.

Central LA is also home to Hollywood – the neighborhood that put American pop culture on the world map – and historic areas like Elysian Park, Echo Park, Silver Lake, Los Feliz, Koreatown, Miracle Mile, Fairfax, and West Hollywood, which house LA's most creative, cosmopolitan residents and iconic LA institutions like Griffith Park, Dodger Stadium, Walt Disney Concert Hall, Hollywood Bowl, LACMA, the Broad, Grand Central Market, and LA's Farmers Market.

Having lived in this part of city since 2012, I'm admittedly partial to Central LA. Sure, it feels chaotic at times, but even ${new Date().getFullYear() - 2012} years later, I'm still amazed to discover – and re-discover – places and new experiences to have, just within the 60 or so square miles around home.
