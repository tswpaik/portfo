Located on the coast of Ishikawa prefecture in the famously remote Hokuriku region of Chubu, Kanazawa (金沢) was once a challenging and time-consuming place to get to, especially for travelers setting out from Tokyo. But with the extension of the Hokuriku Shinkansen in 2015, this hidden gem of a city has quickly gained the attention of both domestic and international travelers. Long known in Japan for its unusually well-preserved traditional districts, arts, and crafts, Kanazawa is often compared to Kyoto in how prevalent and authentically preserved these traditional elements are throughout the city, sometimes even called "Little Kyoto," but don't be misled by the well-meant nickname – not simply a replica of Kyoto, here you'll get to experience an offering of authentically traditional Japan, unique to the culture and history of Kanazawa and the Hokuriku region, all the while avoiding Kyoto's Disneyland-like crowds.

The city became significant in the late 16th century as a major Samurai castle town of the Maeda clan . Ruled from Kanazawa Castle , the domain became one of the wealthiest in Japan during Edo Period (1603-1868) , allowing the feudal lords to patronize traditional arts and craftsmanship – most notably, the production of Japanese gold leaf used in temples, crafts, and even cuisine as decoration. 400 years later, 98% of all gold leaf made in Japan today still comes from Kanazawa according to the Japanese governement . Morever, because the city did not see major destruction throughout history, many original districts and cultural traditions remain well-preserved, serving as one of a handful of cities in Japan that still showcases physical proof of its heritage as a prosperous feudal capital.

Vibes at a Glance

Favorite Stuff to Do

  • Stay at a Machiya: Similar to Kyoto, Kanazawa is known for well-preserved Edo Period Machiya, traditional two-story Japanese wooden townhouses. Unlike Kyoto, however, staying at one – even in an authentic Machiya preservation district like Kazuemachi Chaya-gai – can be done at a comparatively reasonable price and offers a more authentic experience being surrounded only by other Machiya, many of which are still used as Chaya, teahouses where Geisha perform traditional music and dance.
  • Explore Kanazawa's Chaya-gai: Kanazawa has three authentic Edo Period Chaya-gai, Higashi Chaya-gai, Nishi Chaya-gai, and Kazuemachi. While Higashi is the most famous and largest, Kazuemachi, in particular, is located right on the Asano River with a riverfront walkway called Kyouka-no-michi, lined with Sakura trees on one side and beautiful Chaya on the other. It also has atmospheric narrow back alleys where you can admire the detail of the buildings without the bigger crowds at Higashi Chaya-gai.
  • Stroll around Kenroku-en, Kanazawa Castle, and Oyama Shrine: Kanazawa's main sights are luckily close to each other, making it easy to take a leisurely stroll across all of them. The three to explore thoroughly are Kenroku-en, possibly the most impressive of Japan's famed "Three Great Gardens," Kanazawa's unique castle, and Oyama Shrine, known for its elaborate tower-like gate with stained glass windows that was once used as a lighthouse, a truly unique blend of Japanese, Chinese, and Dutch architectural elements within a single structure.
  • Experience Kanazawa's proud local specialties: Kanazawa's traditional crafts and fresh seafood are known throughout Japan. If you don't want to go out of your way, make it easy on yourself and eat at one (or several) of the 200 or so stalls at the 300-year-old Omicho Market, and shop at Hyakubangai a massive underground shopping area in Kanazawa Station – a great one-stop shop for souvenirs, local crafts, and desserts.
