As Japan's ancient capital for over a thousand years, Kyoto (京都) is the historic heart of the country. Originally known as Heian-kyo, the city has been home to Japan's royalty and courtly culture since Heian Period (794-1185) through the end of Edo Period (1603-1868) , when the capital moved to modern-day Tokyo. Nicknamed a "city of 10,000 shrines & temples" highlighting its scale, Kyoto is home to thousands of authentic and well-preserved traditional structures – with 17 of them internationally designated as UNESCO World Heritage Sites . The city's cultural significance, however, extends far beyond its architecture, influencing traditional arts, crafts, festivals, and of course, Japanese cuisine – best exemplified by Obanzai , seasonal Kyoto dishes that reflect the city's refined yet understated culture.

Kyoto is also one of the three major cities that make up the Keihanshin (京阪神) area with 19 million residents, Kansai's largest metropolitan area and second largest in the country. With a population of over a million in city proper, Kyoto is far from a frozen relic of the past. The city will greet you with one of the most modern and cutting-edge train stations in the country, built with the scale to accomodate and impress the overwhelming throngs of domestic and international visitors that flock to the city. With an almost dizzying array of dining and shopping options in Kyoto Station and its modern downtown, Kyoto has something for just about every kind of traveler to Japan.

Vibes at a Glance

Favorite Stuff to Do

  • Orient yourself from Kyoto Tower and spot the top sights: While more compact than major cities like Tokyo or Osaka, Kyoto is by no means a small city. Go up to Kyoto Tower, before or after your visit to the city's top sights, and see where everything is located across the vast basin that is Kyoto. You'll have great views of sights like To-ji and the Hongan-ji temples nestled among modern buildings, Kiyomizu-dera on the hills, and even Osaka's skyline.
  • Walk out from Kyoto station to nearby Hongan-ji temples: Just a five-minute walk from Kyoto Station, Higashi ("East") and Nishi ("West") Hongan-ji are two of the most classic and architecturally detailed Japanese buddhist temples in Kyoto. I consider the pair somewhat of a hidden gem since most tourists tend to head straight to temples and shrines with the most unique features, which means you'll find these two less crowded and authentically serene. Fun fact: LA's Little Tokyo has a Higashi and Nishi Hongan-ji, paying tribute to Kyoto's two head temples.
  • Stroll Kyoto's atmospheric waterways: As you begin to explore Kyoto, you'll undoubtedly come across the Kamo River, which splits the downtown area along Shijo-dori into Gion to its east, and the more modern shopping/dining district from Kawaramachi-dori to Karasuma-dori to its west. The river itself has wide walkable banks enjoyed by locals and tourists alike, often with live music of busking performers in the background. Over on Gion-side, stray a block north from Shijo-dori to find Shirakawa Canal lined with teahouses from Edo Period and picturesque bridges.
  • See majestic temples and shrines illuminated at night: Limited to specific times during spring and fall when evening temperatures are mild and the trees are flowering or turning red, a handful of the city's most famous temples and shrines will meticulously install lighting to illuminate their structures and gardens, extending open hours into the evening. With its grand architecture and expansive hillside temple grounds full of beautiful trees, Kiyomizu-dera stands out for this "light-up" experience. Brace yourself for the crowds, but this unique experience is well worth it.
  • Enjoy Kyoto's peaceful nature: Located west of Kyoto's urban core, Arashiyama is home to picturesque nature surrounded by mountains, a large river, and about 4,000 acres of bamboo forests, and will feel like a true escape from the still mostly urban landscape of Kyoto. Tetsugaku no Michi, "the Philosopher's Path," on the other hand, is a scenic 1.25-mile walkway, still within the city, that follows a stone-paved canal lined with hundreds of Sakura trees along the hillside of Higashiyama. It's a great way to travel slowly from sites like Ginkaku-ji to Nanzen-ji while taking in the beauty of local city streets and its understated nature.
  • Go listen to jazz in Pontocho: There's always a spot for jazz fans in Japan, whether you're in Tokyo or Kyoto. In one of the buildings in Pontocho alley, known for its 17th-century Machiya storefronts, you'll find Hello Dolly, a half-a-century-old jazz & whiskey bar that has been a local favorite for decades. Go just before opening and wait for a shot at the one window seat with views of the Kamo River.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • Downtown (Shijo-dori): Kyoto's downtown generally refers to areas surrounding Shijo-dori, the city's busiest east-west thoroughfare, and has two districts with different vibes. The stretch between Karasuma-dori and Kawaramachi-dori feels modern and urban, while the stretch east of the Kamo River leading up to Yasaka Shrine – called Gion – has the traditional atmosphere you'd expect of Kyoto. Just a few blocks from Shijo-dori are Nishiki Market, a covered street lined with food vendors perfect for small bites, and Pontocho, an atmospheric alley with intimate restaurants and cozy bars along Kyoto's famous Kamo River.
  • Kyoto Station: As you might expect of any large Japanese train station, Kyoto Station has several different malls and indoor shopping/dining wings within the station. The two best areas to comb through, in my experience, are the shared restaurant floor (11F) of Porta and Isetan, accessed from the main (north) entrance of the station, and the restaurants on the first floor of the Shinkansen-side (south) entrance of the station. Wherever you decide to eat, be sure not to miss the station's expansive outdoor atrium and animated LED grand staircase – a modern architectural gem that embodies the city's age-old attention to detail.
