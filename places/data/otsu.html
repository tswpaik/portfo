Just a 10-minute train ride from Kyoto by local commuter lines, Otsu (大津) – the capital of Shiga prefecture – is one of Japan's best-kept secrets, and now easily my favorite place as a basecamp for enjoying the Kansai region as a whole. Flying under the radar thanks to its world-famous neighbor, Otsu sees significantly fewer crowds compared to Kyoto, letting you experience its rich cultural heritage in peace, uninterrupted by over-tourism. Despite its proximity to Kyoto, Otsu has a distinct identity as the heart of an ancient province called Omi , historically serving as a political, cultural, and trading hub on the critical Tokaido route and even as the imperial capital temporarily in the 7th century. What really sets Otsu apart from most of the country, however, is its access to Lake Biwa , Japan's largest lake by far. Shaped like a traditional instrument known as a Biwa , the lake starts with a narrower portion in the south around Otsu and expands like a balloon as it stretches north covering over 260 square miles, a surface area larger than the urban core of Tokyo and three times the size of Osaka. Estimated to be about 4 million years old, Lake Biwa is also one of the oldest lakes in the world, with a unique and ancient biodiversity and, of course, has historically served as the primary source of fresh water, not only for Otsu and Shiga prefecture but all of Kansai. Today, the lake supplies nearly all of Kyoto's tap water through the historic Lake Biwa Canal built in 1890, a critical Meiji-era (1868-1912) engineering project that is widely recognized to have saved the declining economy of modern Kyoto after the capital was moved to Tokyo.

As the largest and most historic city on the lake, Otsu culturally pays tribute to its grand nature, often referring to it the "Mother Lake" . Surrounding Lake Biwa are the city's most significant and striking temples and shrines, with several offering sweeping views of the lake, like the historic Mii-dera (672) – home to 64 National Treasures – and Enryaku-ji (788) – a UNESCO World Heritage Site of Mount Hiei , one of the most important mountains in Japanese Buddhism alongside Wakayama's Mount Koya. The natural beauty of these lakeside temples and shrines were once famous enough to become a central subject of traditional Japanese art – immortalized as the Omi Hakkei or "Eight Views of Omi," an 18th century Ukiyo-e series painted by famed artists like Hiroshige . You can still visit most of these places today and marvel at Lake Biwa's scenery that inspired Japanese artists of the past. Some personal favorites:

  • Mangetsu-ji Ukimido (995) : a one-of-a-kind "Floating Hall" located in the lake itself around Otsu's Katata region
  • Ishiyama-dera (747) : a temple that sits on a stone mountain, where 11th-century author Lady Murasaki wrote the world-famous novel "Tale of Genji ," said to have been inspired by the full moon over Lake Biwa
  • Karasaki Jinja (692) : known for its once majestic, but now declining lakeside pine, which provided a seed planted in 1837 at Kanazawa's Kenroku-en, one of the Three Great Gardens of Japan , giving rise to the garden's centerpiece named "Karasaki Pine"

Vibes at a Glance

Favorite Stuff to Do

  • Explore the Otsukyo Area: Compared to Otsu Station on the JR Biwako line, Otsukyo Station on the JR Kosei line is a more local entry point into Otsu. It's a small station, but sights like Mii-dera and Omi Jingu are nearby, and you can also get a taste of the local Otsu life in this primarily residential area. Stroll on the shoreline in Yanagasaki, where you can spot sail boats on the lake, or drop by Branch Otsukyo, a relaxed outdoor mall with shopping and dining. Best of all, check out the Otsu City Museum of History, a dated city building but with truly fascinating exhibitions about Otsu's storied history.
  • Explore Otsu's historic far ends on the local Keihan line: Once you've arrived to Otsu on a JR line from Kyoto, plan to explore the two most historic areas of the city using the Keihan Ishiyama Sakamoto street car line. Ishiyama, the east end of the city is home to the famed Seta River and Ishiyama-dera, while the north end of the city, Sakamoto, is full of hillside temples, shrines, quaint restaurants and a cable car station that will take you up to Mount Hiei, where Enryaku-ji sits. This local line runs on the street, giving you a chance to peak at the cityscape of Otsu while in transit.
  • Take a canal boat cruise between Lake Biwa and Kyoto: The historic Lake Biwa Canal offers guided history tours by boat, highlighting the architectural details of the canal and the history surrounding its construction. The guide will give the tour in Japanese, but not to worry, English audio guide devices are available. The boat also has a glass ceiling so if you're lucky enough to be there in Fall or Spring, you'll see the canal famously canopied with fall foliage or Sakura. But be warned and prepared: the draft in the canal is no joke.
  • Do day trips around Northern Lake Biwa: Otsu serves as a great base for exploring the rest of Shiga prefecture surrounding Lake Biwa, including the expansive northern portion of the lake. The JR Kosei ("Lake West") line can take you north along the western shores of the lake to places like Ogoto Onsen, Biwako Valley/Terrace, and Shirahige Shrine, while the JR Biwako line serves smaller cities on the northeastern shores like Omihachiman, Hikone, and Nagahama, a port town where you can board a cruise on the ocean-like northside of the lake and head to Chikubu Island, home to a complex with 1,300-year-old temples, shrines, and several National Treasures.
